Leave the mouse in the drawer

Your editor is modal, your multiplexer has a prefix and your window manager tiles. Every tool on the desk speaks the same keyboard language, and your hands never leave the home row. Then you open a browser.

The browser still assumes a pointer. Reading means a scroll wheel, following a link means aiming, and a second page means another window to drag around. Small things, but each one breaks the flow you built in the terminal.

A modal browser fixes this the way Vim did: j and k to read, f to put a label on every link, / to search. The window itself works like tmux: a prefix, then a key, to split it into panes or jump between workspaces.

Reading without a wheel

Scrolling by line is calmer than flinging a wheel. d and u move half a page, gg and G go to the ends, and a count repeats a motion, exactly as in the editor. The page never sees these keys; a text field still gets every one of them. 

j  k      scroll a line
d  u      half a page
gg G      top, bottom
H  L      back, forward

Clicking without aiming

Link hints are the trick that makes the rest possible. Press f and every link on screen gets a short label from the home row. Type the label and the link is followed; targets near the top get the shortest labels, so the common case is two keys.

The fastest pointer is the one you never have to pick up.

Searching without a dialog

/ opens a search at the foot of the page that highlights as you type and counts the matches. Enter keeps the match, n and N walk through the rest, wrapping at the ends. Smartcase, of course.

One keymap, every tool

None of this is new. It is the same small set of habits, finally applied to the one window that ignored them. Every key is yours to remap, and the defaults stay out of each other's way.
